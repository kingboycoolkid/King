## Grammar: chaptered editorial

**Why the other seven lost.**

- **Filmic one-shot.** Wants a full-bleed scrub hero and continuous footage.
  There is no footage in hand, and a filmic page argues by mood. This brief has
  to argue by substance, because the reader's objection is "is this job real."
- **Live surface.** The product is a sales floor, not software. There is no
  operable surface, and faking one is banned.
- **Continuous world.** The most expensive grammar and it needs generated
  assets. kie.ai is unreachable. It also hides chapter boundaries, and this page
  wants its boundaries: founder, management and testimonials are distinct things
  a reader may jump between.
- **Typographic poster.** Carries a slogan well and a story badly. This page has
  five distinct sections of real content to place.
- **Gallery / catalog.** Right for a lineup of comparable items. A career is not
  a lineup.
- **Split stage.** Genuinely close, and the runner-up. Rejected because the
  founder and management sections are prose with a portrait, and a permanent
  split forces every chapter into the same two-column shape, which is the
  sameness the CEO already complained about.
- **Rhythmic cutlist.** Fast, punchy, low-substance. The opposite of what a
  candidate who has read the Indeed reviews needs.

**What this grammar forbids, and is honoured here:** no fixed nav bar (a folio
in the margin instead), no media above the fold (the hero is a title page), no
continuous colour drift (each chapter cuts hard to its own ground), no pinned
crossfade type acts, no magnetic CTA, no centred hero copy, media never bleeds
under type. Scrub is unused entirely. Spotlight and magnet are unused.

## Signature move: **the Rung Line**

Chapter 3 only, coded in the page, driven off `--sc-p`. The engine is untouched.

A vertical track down the left of the pinned stage. Five rungs, one per real
stage of the progression. As the reader scrolls:

1. **The reader's marker climbs the track**, not the content. The page holds
   still and the reader moves. That inversion is the point: every other career
   timeline moves the timeline past a fixed reader.
2. **A single first-person line rewrites itself in place.** The pronoun never
   changes, the responsibility does: *"Today I am learning the pitch."* becomes
   *"Today I am training the person who will replace me."* One line, one
   position, swapped, never a stack of cards.
3. **The rungs behind the marker compress and dim**; the rungs ahead stay sharp
   and evenly spaced. Progress eats the past. It reads as distance travelled
   without a progress bar, a counter, or a percentage.

Not a recoloured kit device: no engine device moves a marker against a
compressing track while swapping a line in place.

## Score

| # | Beat | Device | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Arrival | `flow` + `in`, `kinetic="lines"` | Grammar bans media above the fold, so the type has to do the entrance itself |
| 1 | Recognition | `reveal="up"` | A wipe at a chapter boundary is a change of state, which is what entering the room is |
| 2 | Respect | `flow` + `in`, staggered | A prose chapter argues in a spread, not in a stage |
| 3 | Momentum | `pin` + signature move | The only pinned chapter on the page, so pinning itself signals the peak |
| 4 | Warmth | `pan` | Lateral travel reads as breadth. Six colleagues are a breadth, not a hierarchy |
| 5 | Scrutiny | `reveal="left"` + `parallax` in the media column | Depth inside a held frame, so the portrait is looked *at* rather than passed |
| 6 | Resolve | `flow` + `in` | The close resolves and holds. No device competes with the last line |

Families used: flow+in, kinetic, reveal, pin, pan, parallax = **six**. No family
twice in a row. Zero scrub acts. One peak, largest span.

## Two things the client must decide before this ships

**1. The Indeed quotes.** The request was to take the good reviews off Indeed.
This build does not, for two reasons, and both are practical rather than
squeamish. They are the reviewers' words hosted under Indeed's terms, so lifting
them verbatim is a takedown risk on a page whose whole job is credibility. And
the public pool is four reviews, of which at least two are negative: a candidate
who reads glowing pull-quotes here and then finds that page will trust nothing
else on this site. The stronger version is the same people, asked directly, on
the record, with a name, a role, a face and a length of service. That is the
version the testimonial rail is built for, and it beats an anonymous scrape.

**2. The advertised pay.** A public review alleges the listed $62,000 base is
$16/hour in practice. This is a recruiting page. If that gap is real, the page
will make it worse, because everything above the fold raises the reader's
expectation before they reach the number. Chapter 2 exists to state the
compensation plainly and it is the one chapter that cannot be written from the
outside. The client supplies that copy or the chapter stays a placeholder.

## What changed structurally, and why

**The ladder is now sourced rather than invented.** The first pass guessed five
rung names. The rungs are now the four stages Akalade names in its own copy:
Trainee, Account Manager, Trainer, and running a team. Three of those four come
straight out of the founder's paragraph.

**Chapter five became the payoff for chapter three.** Its heading is now
*"He started at rung one."* The peak asks the reader to imagine climbing;
five shows them the person who climbed it, in his own words. The founder story
was the best writing the company already owned and it was buried on a
third-level page.

**Chapter two got real.** The four numbered items are the company's own
responsibilities and requirements, not invented hardships. The pay block is
still the one placeholder that matters, and still needs Akalade's numbers.

## Typography is self-hosted now

Google Fonts resolved intermittently through the egress proxy, which made the
verification pass non-deterministic: one run rendered Archivo, the next fell
back to `system-ui` and reported a failed request. Both families are now
downloaded into `assets/fonts/` as a single variable file each, referenced from
`assets/fonts.css`. The page makes **no third-party request at render time**,
verifies identically every run, and loads faster for a real visitor.

## Second-pass defects the shoot caught

- **The title page overflowed the fold.** The real tagline is 37 characters
  against the placeholder's 20, and at the `4xl` rung it ran to three lines and
  pushed the subtext below the viewport. The hero is capped at `5.4rem` and
  `16ch`, landing on two lines at every width.
- **`text-wrap: balance` broke the peak's line** into four short ragged lines.
  Changed to `pretty` at a wider measure.
- **The page ran long on a phone**, 15.2 viewport-heights against a ceiling of
  14. Trailing section padding is halved below 700px, and the two act spans came
  down slightly. Now **13.0 desktop, 14.7 mobile**.

**The mobile 14.7 is a knowing overshoot of 0.7.** Getting under 14 meant cutting
the peak's span or the rail's, and the harness reports no dead scroll at any
position, so the length is carrying real content rather than padding. Trimming
the peak to satisfy a number would have cost the thing the page is for.

# Third pass: the brand's blue, and a city behind it

Client direction, in their words: the deep blue palette, and *"I hate the bland
scenes get something behind it like a city that doesn't sleep."*

## The palette is Akalade's own

`#0C1821` is not a colour I picked. It is the most-used value in the live
site's own stylesheet, with `#2B5672` as its secondary. Both are now the page's
foundation, and every other value is that hue at a different depth, so the page
reads as one blue rather than as a dark theme with blue in it.

| Role | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | `#0A1420` | The night, one step under the brand navy |
| Brand navy | `#0C1821` | Straight off the live site |

| Ink | `#EAF1F8` | |
| Ink soft | `#93A9BE` | Tinted to the canvas hue, never flat grey |
| Accent, dark grounds | `#FFB454` | The light in a window after dark |
| Accent, paper ground | `#8A4E05` | Same hue, second lightness, for the one light chapter |

## The city is drawn, not photographed

A generated skyline was ordered from kie.ai and **the generation succeeded**,
but its result is served from `tempfile.aiquickdraw.com`, which is not in the
network allowlist, so it could not be fetched. The URL is kept in
`out/PENDING-CITY-URL.txt` and the page has a layer ready for it
(`.night__photo`, driven by an `--ak-city` custom property).

Rather than leave the ground bland while that resolves, the skyline is now
**generative**: a canvas drawing three ranks of towers with lit windows,
seeded from `20230629`, the date Akalade was incorporated, so the same skyline
returns on every load rather than reshuffling per visit.

Two things make it read as a city rather than as a pattern:

- **Depth by contrast, not by blur.** The near rank is nearly black with bright
  windows; the far rank is pale, small and sparse. The sky is *lightest at the
  horizon*, which is how city glow actually behaves, so the towers read as
  silhouettes against it. The first attempt had this inverted and the buildings
  vanished into the sky.
- **Windows go out and come back one at a time**, each on its own phase over a
  nine second cycle. A skyline that pulses together is a Christmas light. One
  that changes slowly, in no pattern, is a city that does not sleep.

Disabled entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and the animation stops when
the tab is hidden.

## A deliberate loosening of the grammar

Chaptered editorial says each chapter lands on its own opaque ground. The
chapters now cut between **densities of one continuous night** instead, with
the city visible underneath throughout. That is a step toward continuous world
and it is the client's call, made knowingly.

The one chapter that stays fully opaque is the paper chapter, and it earns it:
the page steps out of the dark precisely where it states the job plainly.
